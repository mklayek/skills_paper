# Title, Authors and Keywords — template

**Purpose & writing logic**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The title, author block, and keyword line together form the paper's discovery surface. Across the three Goodman-lineage papers the title always names the method and the target explicitly, so the paper is findable by a geophysicist searching for "GPR" and by an archaeologist searching for "burial mound" alike. Two title shapes recur. The first is the bare noun-phrase that leads with the technique's output product, as in "GPR Time Slices in Archaeological Prospection," which puts the method-plus-deliverable ahead of any site name. The second is the colon-split shape, where a conceptual clause is followed by a concrete case-study anchor, as in "The Measurement and Analysis of Depth in Archaeological Geophysics: Tests at the Biesterfeldt Site, USA." A third, more narrative variant drives the sentence with a verb, as in "The Search for Subterranean Kofun Burial Chambers... using Ground Penetrating Radar," which signals a discovery story rather than a neutral technical report.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The author block encodes the collaboration itself. The canonical archaeological-GPR team is a triad, which pairs the geophysical-method specialist (usually the lead and the only author carrying full contact details) with a local site authority such as a museum curator, and with a national or regional research-institute archaeologist. That's why the ordering is by role and contribution, not alphabetical. The geophysicist supplies the survey and the interpretation, the site authority supplies legitimate access and ground-truth excavation, and the institute archaeologist supplies the historical framing. Keywords then run from the broadest disciplinary term down to the most specific, or the reverse, but they always cover three registers: a culture-or-period term, the primary method name, and a method-category umbrella.

**When to write / what belongs here**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Write this block first as a placeholder and finalize it last, once the paper's actual scope is settled. The title must name the method and the target class. The author block must reflect real contributions, so never fabricate co-authors to look collaborative. The keyword line belongs here whenever the venue asks for one, which most journals do. In this block you do not write any results, any site detail beyond the naming level, or any methodological justification.

**Recommended paragraph flow**

1. Compose the title so it names the geophysical method and the archaeological target, choosing the noun-phrase, colon-split, or narrative-verb shape to match the venue.
2. List authors in contribution order, marking the corresponding author and attaching full contact details to that one author only.
3. Attach numbered affiliations, mixing academic, museum, government, and private-consultant institutions where that reflects the real team.
4. Write three to six keywords spanning a culture-or-period term, the method, and a method-category umbrella.

**Expected scientific content**

- The full method name spelled out once, with its acronym in parentheses on first use if the venue allows an acronym in the title.
- The archaeological target class, such as burial mounds, chambers, or ditches, named at the class level.
- The region or country, so the study is geographically locatable.
- Author roles and affiliations that honestly reflect the geophysicist-plus-site-authority-plus-institute triad.
- Three to six keywords covering period or culture, method, and method category.

**Citation strategy**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;No in-text citations appear in the title, author, or keyword block. The only reference-like markers are the affiliation footnotes and the corresponding-author email, which are structural rather than bibliographic.

**Figure/table references**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;None. No figures or tables are cited in this block.

**Tense & voice**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The title is a tenseless noun phrase or, in the narrative variant, a nominalized verb phrase such as "The Search for." Author and keyword lines carry no verbs. The register is impersonal throughout, with no first person anywhere.

**Common phrases / sentence openers**

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Real title and keyword patterns quoted from the analyses:

- "GPR Time Slices in Archaeological Prospection"
- "The Measurement and Analysis of Depth in Archaeological Geophysics: Tests at the Biesterfeldt Site, USA"
- "The Search for Subterranean Kofun Burial Chambers on the Island of Kyushu, Japan using Ground Penetrating Radar"
- "Japanese kofun burial chambers, ground penetrating radar, subsurface remote sensing" (keyword line)
- "Magnetic methods; electrical methods; ground-penetrating radar; three-dimensional; downhole measurements; direct-push colour" (keyword line)

**Quality checklist**

- [ ] The title names both the geophysical method and the archaeological target class.
- [ ] The title shape (noun-phrase, colon-split, or narrative-verb) matches the target venue's register.
- [ ] The method acronym, if used, is expanded on first appearance.
- [ ] Authors are ordered by contribution, with the corresponding author marked and given contact details.
- [ ] Affiliations reflect the real institutional mix and no co-author is fabricated.
- [ ] Keywords span a period-or-culture term, the method, and a method-category umbrella.
- [ ] No results, site detail, or methodology leak into this block.

**Short worked example**

> Ground Penetrating Radar Detection of Subterranean Kofun Chambers: Tests at the Saitobaru Mounds, Miyazaki, Japan
>
> D. Author\*, Geophysicist, Geophysical Prospection Laboratory, Springfield, USA, author@example.org
> N. Curator, Curator, Saitobaru Archaeological Museum, Miyazaki, Japan
> Y. Institute, Archaeologist, National Cultural Properties Research Institute, Japan
>
> Keywords: Kofun burial mounds; ground penetrating radar; subsurface remote sensing
