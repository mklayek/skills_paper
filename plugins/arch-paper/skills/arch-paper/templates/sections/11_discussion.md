# Discussion — template

## 3. Purpose & writing logic

The Discussion answers the paper's real question, which is not what was found at this one site but what the finding tells us generally about doing archaeological geophysics. Across the three Goodman papers the Discussion synthesizes across all techniques and all site areas, returns to the framing set up in the introduction or the background theory, and closes the loop between what was promised and what was delivered. The 2011 Biesterfeldt paper is the clearest model, since its fused Discussion and Conclusions moves technique by technique, giving each a verdict paragraph that pairs a general capability claim with an immediate scope-limiting caveat drawn from a counter-example in the same paper. The 1995 paper folds a "defend and extend" discussion into its Conclusions, spending a full passage rebutting a real methodological skepticism using its own evidence. The 2014 paper distributes its discussion inline, so for a journal build the template pulls that scattered content into an explicit section.

So the writing logic is candor and generalization. The Discussion never lets a single success stand as a general claim. Every strong positive result is immediately followed by the limit, often citing the failure case by name. Favorable outcomes get attributed partly to site conditions and luck, not only to technique quality, which is the honesty marker that separates a rigorous comparison paper from a promotional one. Where two methods agree or disagree, the Discussion gives the physical reason, not just the observation.

## 4. When to write / what belongs here

Write the Discussion once the full set of Results and Interpretations exists, since it reasons across all of them. What belongs here is cross-technique synthesis, the verdict on each method with its scope caveat, the practical field-workflow payoff such as excavation guidance and budgeting, the mechanistic explanation for correlations and non-correlations between methods, the candor paragraph about site conditions and luck, and the forward-looking recommendations for future practitioners. Comparison to prior work at the same or a comparable site belongs here too.

What does not belong here is a fresh anomaly reading not already reported, or a restatement of Results without added synthesis. Generally the Discussion should feel like a level up in altitude from Results, moving from this feature to this method to this practice. Where a shorter paper fuses Discussion and Conclusions into one section, keep the synthesis and verdicts here and let the final closing generalization carry the conclusions load.

## 5. Recommended paragraph flow

1. Open with a retrospective summary claim, in present perfect, naming the two or three top-level payoffs the work delivered.
2. Give a verdict paragraph per technique, pairing a general capability claim with an explicit scope-limiting caveat that cites the counter-example.
3. State the practical field-workflow payoff, described concretely as excavation guidance, test-unit placement, or budgeting.
4. Synthesize across techniques, giving the physical mechanism for why methods agreed or disagreed.
5. Add the candor paragraph, attributing favorable outcomes partly to site conditions and stating when the approach would not have worked.
6. Compare to prior work at the same or a comparable site.
7. Offer forward-looking recommendations, which are concrete technical suggestions for future practitioners.
8. Close by returning to the paper's opening framing and stating the generalizable takeaway.

## 8. Citation strategy

The Discussion cites in three modes. It cites the authors' own prior work at the site for continuity, showing the paper extends an ongoing program rather than starting fresh. It cites an independent comparable study when placing this work against the wider field, which the 2014 paper does by pointing to the Tenri University keyhole-tomb GPR work. It cites foundational theory when a mechanistic claim about method behavior needs an authority, as with potential-field theory. Density here is moderate, well below the background section but above Results.

## 9. Figure/table references typical here

The Discussion usually references figures already introduced rather than presenting new ones, calling back to the composite validation figure or the multi-track comparison figure to anchor a cross-technique verdict. A synthesis or interpreted cross-section figure can appear here to summarize the site-wide picture. Tables are uncommon in this genre, though a longer journal build may add a method-comparison summary table that scores each technique against each sub-problem, which suits the verdict-paragraph structure well.

## 10. Tense & voice guidance

Use present perfect for cumulative claims about what the work achieved, which is "have yielded," "have demonstrated." Use present tense for generalizations about method behavior, which is "downhole geophysics detects thin strata that at-surface methods cannot." Use conditional and modal verbs for recommendations, which is "it would be beneficial to," "future surveys might." The collective "we" is acceptable and useful here at moments of authorial judgment and candor, as in "we were fortunate that the soil was not stony." Keep procedural statements passive.

## 11. Common phrases / sentence openers

- "This technique, however, cannot be expected to produce this level of detail in every instance."
- "The downhole tests demonstrated that there are thin strata or small features that can be detected only with downhole geophysics."
- "While the lack of strong correlation may appear to make the data less reliable, it also indicates that the readings were independent."
- "Biesterfeldt was a good site for borehole geophysics; this was partly because..."
- "We were fortunate that the soil was not stoney, hard or water-saturated."
- "The tests at the Biesterfeldt site have demonstrated the benefits of combining geophysical depth information with at-surface surveys."

## 13. Short worked example

**Discussion**

The GPR and magnetic tests at Mound Field 7 have yielded new information on hut construction, on the buried enclosure ditch, and on the extent of plough damage across the southern site. The GPR overlay analysis resolved individual roof-support posts at Hut 12, which is a strong result, yet it cannot be expected to perform this well everywhere, since over Hut 20 the same survey missed the central posts entirely and only the downhole susceptibility log recovered them. In practical terms these depth results let the excavation team place test units directly over the confirmed hearth rather than trenching blind, which cut the exposed area substantially. Where susceptibility and viscosity agreed over the hearths, the reason is physical, since both respond to concentrations of fine magnetic grains produced by burning, and in a coarser mineralogical setting that agreement would not hold. This was a good site for borehole work partly by fortune, since the ash-rich soil was neither stony nor waterlogged, without which the coring would have been impossible. Compared with the Tenri keyhole-tomb survey (Edwards and Okita, 2006), the present work adds downhole validation of the at-surface anomalies. For future surveys it would be beneficial to standardize the borehole diameter and to sample at finer depth intervals over suspected floors. These tests have demonstrated the benefit of combining depth-focused measurements with area survey in order to define features, read their stratigraphy, and guide the dig.
