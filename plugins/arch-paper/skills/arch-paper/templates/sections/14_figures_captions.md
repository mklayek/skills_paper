# Figures & Captions — template

## Purpose & writing logic

    Figures carry the evidentiary weight of an archaeological-GPR paper. All three source papers are figure-dominant: Goodman et al. (1995) has 4 figures and no tables, Dalan et al. (2011) leans on multi-track dashboard figures in place of tables, and Goodman et al. (2014) runs a 5:4 ratio of figure pages to text pages. The writing logic follows from that weight. A caption is not a label. It is a self-sufficient mini-paragraph that states both what the figure shows and what it means, and it carries the paper's precise numeric findings so that a reader skimming only the figures and captions can reconstruct the core results. Every caption in the source papers is argumentative: it names the anomaly, gives its measured dimension, and states the interpretation, not just the site and depth range.

    The second logic is archetype coverage. A strong case-study paper cycles through a recognizable set of figure archetypes: a regional context map to orient the reader, a radargram (vertical profile) showing raw reflections, a time-slice or depth-slice map draped over a site plan or aerial or GIS base, a 3D isosurface rendering, and a validation figure pairing the geophysical interpretation against an excavation photograph. Aim to include at least one of each.

## When to write / what belongs here

    Draft each caption at the moment you place its figure, then revisit all captions together at the end to enforce a uniform structure and to confirm every measured dimension in the caption matches the number in the running text. A caption belongs to exactly one figure and one first in-text reference. What belongs in a caption: a first descriptive sentence identifying the data type, site, and depth or date range with no interpretation yet, then one or more sentences of interpretive payload including at least one measured dimension. For composite or multi-panel figures, add one sentence per component walked through in the same order as the panel layout, plus a legend gloss (what the warm and cool colors mean, what the symbols mean, how the image was produced).

    Decide up front whether the target venue wants figures inline near their first citation (modern journal norm) or batched at the end (proceedings and camera-ready norm, as in Goodman et al., 2014). The choice changes referencing rhythm but not caption content.

## Fill-in skeleton

In-text reference (two modes, pick per role):

```
{{Illustrative figure, trailing parenthetical}}
... bounded by tall mountains ({{Figure N}}).

{{Primary-result figure, fronted subject}}
Shown in {{Figure N}} {{is/are}} {{the primary result: e.g., a georeferenced depth slice from 1.1-1.4 m}} overlain on {{base image}}.

{{Cross-referenced pair}}
A comparison of {{Figure N}} and {{Figure M}} shows that {{finding}}.
```

Caption (single-panel):

```
Figure {{N}}. {{Descriptive sentence: data type, site name, depth or date range, no interpretation}}. {{Interpretive sentence: what the anomaly is, with at least one measured dimension, e.g., "a buried moat approximately 22 m in diameter"}}. {{Optional legend gloss: color/symbol meaning or production method}}.
```

Caption (composite / multi-panel):

```
Figure {{N}}. {{Identification: what and where}}. {{Panel 1 sentence: what the radargram/first panel shows}}. {{Panel 2 sentence: what the isosurface/second panel shows}}. {{Panel 3 sentence: what the excavation photo/third panel shows}}. {{Optional: cross-reference to another figure showing the same feature via a different data type}}.
```

    Inline instruction: use "Figure N" spelled out in the body text. Captions may use the journal's caption label style (some journals print "Fig. N"; Goodman et al., 1995 printed "Fig 1" on the plate while spelling "Figure 1" in the body, a split worth avoiding). State every dimension in the caption with its unit and put an approximate qualifier ("approximately," "about") in front of any figure-measured value.

## Expected content

    A complete figure set for a case-study paper covers the five archetypes: (1) regional map placing the site within its country or region, (2) at least one radargram showing raw vertical reflections with interpretive labels overlaid, (3) at least one time-slice or depth-slice planview map, ideally draped over a site plan, aerial photo, or Google Earth base, (4) a 3D isosurface or volume rendering of the strongest reflectors, and (5) a validation figure pairing the GPR interpretation with an excavation photograph or an independent ground-truth source. Each caption states the figure content, the interpretation, and at least one number. Multi-instrument papers may add a dashboard figure combining several data tracks plus a photo plus a color column, with a per-track credit block beneath (Dalan et al., 2011).

## Style / format rules

- Captions are self-sufficient mini-paragraphs stating both what the figure shows and its measured dimensions.
- First sentence is purely descriptive; interpretation follows in the next sentence, never the first.
- Put every measured dimension into the caption with its unit, prefixed by "approximately" or "about" for image-read values.
- Body text spells "Figure N" in full. Illustrative figures get trailing parenthetical citations; primary-result figures get a fronted "Shown in Figure N is/are..." construction.
- Composite figures get one sentence per panel in layout order, plus a legend gloss.
- Use US English. No em dashes in captions (recast with a comma or period). Avoid semicolons; split into two sentences instead.
- Captions are running prose, so first-line indent conventions of the body do not apply to the caption block itself, but keep the prose paragraph clean and unindented under the figure.

## Common phrases / real examples

- "Time slices calculated for the Nutubaru burial mound site. The lowest time slice shows a large ring anomaly, which is a buried moat (22 m diameter) that is still left intact beneath farmland." (Goodman et al., 1995, Figure 1)
- "A radar profile taken across the centre of the ring anomaly imaged at the Nutubaru site. Both of the moat radar anomalies are clearly seen on the profile. A (collapsed) burial chamber is also imaged within the moats." (Goodman et al., 1995, Figure 2, radargram archetype)
- "Shallow time slices for House 33 and the ditch area at the Biesterfeldt site. ... Relatively strong reflections are shown by warm colours (red, orange) whereas weak reflections correlate with cool colours (blue, green)." (Dalan et al., 2011, Figure 3, with legend gloss)
- "A radargram profile taken at the Sakamotonoue site shows two closely spaced subterranean chamber burial reflections. An isosurface rendering shows the general size, shape and depth of these chambers. A subsequent excavation photo shows the entrance to chamber burial #2 and the interior." (Goodman et al., 2014, Figure 2, composite validation figure walked panel by panel)
- "The size of the square burial pit on the top of the mound can be estimated from the strong square red anomaly seen on the 1.03 meter depth slice and is about 5 meters." (Goodman et al., 2014, Figure 5, measured dimension carried in the caption)
- In-text, fronted: "Shown in Figure 3 is a georeferenced depth slice from 1.1-1.4 m below the ground surface overlain on a Google Earth photo." (Goodman et al., 2014)

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Every figure is cited at least once in the body text, and the first citation matches the figure's placement or batch order.
- [ ] Each caption's first sentence is descriptive only; interpretation appears in a later sentence.
- [ ] Every measured dimension stated in the running text also appears in the relevant caption, with matching value and unit.
- [ ] The figure set covers the five archetypes: regional map, radargram, time-slice or depth-slice overlay, 3D isosurface, and excavation-validation figure.
- [ ] Composite figures have one caption sentence per panel, in layout order, plus a legend gloss.
- [ ] Illustrative figures use trailing parentheticals; primary-result figures use the fronted "Shown in Figure N" construction.
- [ ] "Figure N" is spelled out in the body text, and the caption label style is consistent across all figures.
- [ ] US English, no em dashes, no stray semicolons in captions.

## Short worked example

    In-text (fronted, primary result): "Shown in Figure 4 is a depth slice at 0.9 to 1.2 m over Mound 12, overlain on the site plan. A ring-shaped reflection approximately 14 m in diameter matches the recorded lodge outline."

    Caption (composite validation figure):

```
Figure 4. Depth slice at 0.9 to 1.2 m below the surface over Mound 12, Saitobaru, draped on the site plan. The ring-shaped reflection, approximately 14 m in diameter, corresponds to the outer wall of a keyhole tomb recorded in the historic survey. The isosurface panel on the right renders the 75 percent strongest reflectors and resolves two internal chambers at about 2.5 m depth. The excavation photograph confirms the northern chamber entrance and its intact stone lintel.
```
